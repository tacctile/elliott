# Nesting and Material Cost

**This item's cost model is structurally different from every other Band A/B/C cut vinyl item on the account and must NOT be cloned onto a future multi-piece item without independent derivation. See Notes and Warnings.**

## Production Layout (Non-Standard Nesting)

Unlike every other cut vinyl item on the account, this item's production layout is not designed for material efficiency. It is designed to guarantee the 5-piece set always cuts, weeds, and masks together, as one file load, on one carrier sheet:

- The five numbers ("24", "30", "35", "40", "45") are **rotated 90°** and nested at a **fixed 4.25" repeating width**, run across the **full 30" roll width** per repeat.
- **Feed pitch: 7.5"** = 6.5" cutout height + 1" margin.
- One full-width repeat produces the complete 5-piece set. There is no multi-across nesting of additional sets within a repeat — the entire 30" roll width is consumed by the single repeat that yields one set.

This is a **deliberate production choice**, not a material-efficiency nest. Its purpose is to guarantee the 5 pieces of one kit are always produced together, on one carrier sheet, in one pass — eliminating any risk of a partial set, a mismatched masking pass, or a lost piece between file load and shipment.

## Why the Nominal Bounding-Box Footprint Is Not Used for Costing

The nominal bounding-box footprint of the production layout is 22.25" × 6.5" = **1.004 sq ft**. This figure does **not** represent true material consumption for this item and must not solely drive pricing:

- It describes the *arrangement* of the five numbers on the carrier sheet, not the linear roll footage actually consumed per set.
- Because the layout consumes the **full 30" roll width** per 7.5" feed-pitch repeat (not a partial width), the true material cost is driven by the feed pitch and the full roll width — not by the bounding-box area of the printed numbers within that repeat.

## Verified Material Cost — $2.86/Set

Derived directly from real roll costs, using the feed-pitch/full-width-consumption method:

| Step | Value |
|------|-------|
| Feed pitch | 7.5" = 0.2083 yd (7.5 ÷ 36) |
| Vinyl roll rate | 30" × 50yd white 3M 180mC @ $616.25 total = $12.325/yd |
| Tape roll rate | 30" × 100yd TransferRite 582U @ $141.86 total = $1.4186/yd |
| **Vinyl cost per set** | 0.2083 yd × $12.325/yd = **$2.5677** |
| **Tape cost per set** | 0.2083 yd × $1.4186/yd = **$0.2955** |
| **Total material cost per set** | $2.5677 + $0.2955 = **$2.8632 → filed at $2.86** |

One full-width repeat = one complete 5-piece set — there is no per-marker division and no multi-across nesting to amortize. **$2.86 is the material cost of the entire 5-piece set**, which is the sellable unit. This is the frontmatter `material_cost_per_unit` figure.

**Do not confuse this with the nominal 1.004 sq ft bounding-box footprint or attempt to re-derive material cost from it.** The two figures (1.004 sq ft nominal footprint vs. $2.86/set true material cost) are independent facts about this item and are not interchangeable.

# Production Process

1. File import/setup — open the Elliott-supplied production-ready DWG (DWG# 3021815, ENG JHP), send to cutter. ~5 min mechanical. NOT billable labor (Core Rule 7, `PRICING_RULES.md` §22).
2. Cut — Roland SG3-300 (or 54" plotter, roll-width dependent), 30" white 3M 180mC roll. All 5 numbers cut together in one 7.5" feed-pitch repeat, full 30" roll width.
3. Weed — remove excess vinyl around all 5 numeral cutouts. Because all 5 numbers load and cut on one carrier sheet, weeding proceeds as a single matched-set operation, not five independent passes.
4. Mask — apply TransferRite 582U application tape (premask required, no paint mask) across the full-width repeat, covering all 5 numbers in one masking pass.
5. Inspect — confirm all 5 markers ("24", "30", "35", "40", "45") are present, correctly cut, and correctly masked before separating from the carrier sheet.
6. Package — collate the 5-piece set together, ship as one kit. **Never split into individual markers for packaging or shipment.**

- **Lamination Passes:** 0 (cut vinyl, no lamination)
- **Cut Runs:** 1 (single repeat produces the full 5-piece set)

# Pricing Derivation

**This session documents and files pricing that was independently derived through a full 4-wave, 6-model AI validation process run externally in ChatHub and synthesized in Claude Chat, then adjusted by Nick's own judgment. This session does NOT re-derive or re-run that validation.**

## Why This Item Does Not Route Through the Category Pricing Profile

`categories/cut-vinyl-3m-180mc.md`'s Band A/B/C Pricing Profile is built entirely on single-label $/sq ft data — every existing data point is one label, one $/sq ft figure, one cost basis. This item is structurally different in two respects the Profile does not model:

1. It is a 5-piece kit sold as one unit, not a single label — there is no single "label" $/sq ft to compute in the way the Profile's routing logic (`sqft = sq_ft_per_label`) expects.
2. Its material cost is driven by full-roll-width consumption at a fixed feed pitch, not by the label's own bounding-box area — the Profile's cost model (length-based vinyl/tape per label, amortized across labels-per-row) does not apply.

Per Rule 15 (`governance/PRICING_RULES.md`), every new item validates against the category Pricing Profile band — but per Rule 16, if an item falls outside the band for a legitimate structural reason, that reason is documented rather than forced into the band. This item is documented here as a structural exception, filed as a `BAND_EXCEPTIONS` entry in `scripts/validate.py`, consistent with the account's existing treatment of other structurally-excluded items (P/N 1257750, P/N ELLIOTT BOOM DECAL).

## Wave 1 — Build Round (6 models)

**Unanimous 6/6**: pricing must be driven by actual production cost ($2.86/set, verified) — not by the nominal bounding-box sq ft figure (1.004 sq ft). All six models independently rejected sq-ft-based pricing as the primary driver, citing the full-roll-width consumption logic as the correct cost basis.

Qty-20 anchors proposed ranged **$14.75–$18.50**. Several models relied on an unverified "5x weeding labor" multiplier — reasoning that weeding five separate numerals takes roughly five times the labor of weeding one label — to justify implied margins of 84–88%. This multiplier was not supported by any measured production data.

## Wave 2 — Destruction Round (6 models)

**Majority (4/6)** converged on a reproducible cost-delta bridge, rather than the Wave 1 weeding-labor multiplier:

- P/N 3010736's $14.50 qty-20 anchor (1.012 sq ft, engine-verified $2.47/label material cost)
- + the verified material cost delta between this item and 3010736: $2.86 − $2.47 = **$0.39**
- = **$14.89**, rounded to the nearest $0.25-compliant (§30) price: **$14.95**

The **"5x weeding labor" multiplier was rejected by the majority** as unverified — no production timing data exists to support a 5x figure specifically, and the resulting 84–88% margins were found inconsistent with the account's observed ~83% material-only margin ceiling (the highest confirmed margin on any Band A item, P/N 3010736 itself, at 83.0%).

## Wave 3 — Buyer Simulation Round (6 models)

**5/6 ESCALATE, 1/6 APPROVE.** The escalation was **not a price objection** — every escalating model reached the same conclusion: this is Elliott's **first order for this item**, with zero purchase history to anchor buyer expectations. The unanimous concern was that the quote must explicitly state:

(a) this is priced as **one complete 5-piece set** — not a per-marker price, and

(b) the **cost-basis logic** — a verified material-cost delta vs. P/N 3010736 — to prevent buyer confusion about why a 5-piece kit prices only modestly above a single label, or a follow-up loop with Sean questioning the structure.

## Wave 4 — Final Synthesis (6 models)

**Unanimous 6/6**: lock the **$14.95 qty-20 anchor** — no override recommended on the anchor itself. Unanimous agreement that the first-order/no-history status identified in Wave 3 makes explicit quote documentation **more** necessary, not less — Wave 4 did not treat Wave 3's escalation as a reason to change the price, only as a reason to document the scope and cost-basis clearly in any Sean-facing communication.

## Nick's Locked Adjustment (Owner Judgment)

Nick reviewed the Wave 4 synthesized $14.95 anchor and applied his own judgment, adjusting the qty 20-49 tier down $0.20 to **$14.75**, and re-shaping the surrounding tier spread (1-9, 10-19, 50-99, 100-199, 200+) — a deviation beyond the raw Wave 4 output. The basis for this adjustment is Elliott's specific historical purchasing pattern on this account: **Elliott has never ordered any item on this account above qty 20**. Given that, Nick weighted the tier structure so that:

- **1-9 and 10-19 carry the real pricing weight** — these are the tiers Elliott's actual order pattern will land in.
- **50-99 through 200+ are filed as informational/theoretical tiers** — present for a complete, professional-looking 6-tier ladder (per `PRICING_RULES.md` §9), but not expected to be transacted against based on Elliott's history.

`override_type: "Owner Judgment"` — this is Nick adjusting the tier spread beyond the AI-synthesized anchor, based on account-specific purchasing-pattern knowledge the validation waves did not have. Per Core Rule 12/13, this override is logged with the engine's original recommendation ($14.95) and Nick's final number ($14.75) here and in frontmatter.
